# Tutorials

The other sections of this site are a reference, organized by topic. The tutorials are a path through it instead: step-by-step Jupyter notebooks that start with nothing installed and end with your own analysis and your own modules. Each tutorial:

- states its goal, its time, what it needs, and its data and their size;
- explains the physics behind each step, linked to [The physics](https://jhputschke.github.io/js-contrib/about/physics/index.md);
- has numbered steps you run yourself;
- ends with a **check your result** cell, which compares against the expected numbers, and with exercises and links to the reference pages.

The notebooks run on your own computer: in the production container, with the xscape wheel on a Mac, or in the analysis environment. The analysis tutorials also run in Google Colab. The pages here show them with their outputs. To run them, see [Setting up](https://jhputschke.github.io/js-contrib/tutorials/setup/index.md).

The outputs shown were produced on an Apple Silicon Mac, in the environment the xscape wheel 0.2.0 is built in, with the hydro on the GPU through Metal. A notebook that needed something else says so at its top. Where a tutorial reads fixed example files, your numbers should match the ones shown. Where it runs X-SCAPE, they can differ slightly from setup to setup.

Work in progress: examples, not vetted physics results

The tutorials and their analyses are work in progress and need further vetting. They are meant mainly as **examples of how to run the simulation and use its outputs**. The data sets are small (one background with five jets, a hundred-odd events, a few O+O events), so the statistics are low, and the definitions and procedures have not been checked to the standard of a publication. Treat every number and figure as an illustration, not as a physics result, and see the [note on the analysis section](https://jhputschke.github.io/js-contrib/analysis/index.md).

The advanced tutorials (*later*) follow once the comparisons with measured data have been tested further; until then the tutorials compare the model only with itself (jet against background, Au+Au against p+p).

## The data

The tutorials read example files from the OSDF with [pelicanfs](https://github.com/PelicanPlatform/pelicanfs), the OSDF's client for Python, with no account needed (tutorial 01 introduces it). The files are streamed as they are read, or downloaded once with `get_data.py`; see [Setting up](https://jhputschke.github.io/js-contrib/tutorials/setup/#3-the-data). The data sets and their contents are listed in [`datasets.json`](https://github.com/jhputschke/js-contrib/blob/main/tutorials/pyjetscape/datasets.json).

## Other contribs

The tutorials live in [`tutorials/<contrib>/`](https://github.com/jhputschke/js-contrib/tree/main/tutorials) in the repository, one folder per contrib. Tutorials for FastHydro and the visualization can follow the same layout.
